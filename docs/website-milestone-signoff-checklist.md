# Before you approve a website milestone: a sign-off checklist for each stage

*By Tarun Kumar · 16 November 2026*

Most website projects are paid for in stages. That part is fine. The problem is how stages get approved. Too often, a milestone invoice is cleared on the strength of a status update: a call, a message, a screen-share that says "design is done" or "the site is ready." A status update is not a deliverable. And once you approve a milestone and release the payment, that approval is hard to undo. The money is gone, and the project moves to the next stage on top of whatever you accepted.

Use this file as a website project milestone checklist. Each section tells you what you should be holding at that stage, what to check, and one red flag to watch for.

## Discovery

What you should be holding: a short written summary of your business, your customers, and what the site must achieve.

- [ ] The summary exists in writing, not just as notes from a call.
- [ ] It names who the site is for and what those visitors should do: call, buy, book, or send an enquiry.
- [ ] It states what happens to your existing site, content, and domain, if you have them.
- [ ] Your questions, and the studio's answers, are recorded somewhere you can find later.
- [ ] The studio asked you questions about your business instead of only taking instructions.

Red flag: the studio wants to skip discovery and start designing in the same week you first spoke.

## Scope

What you should be holding: a scope document you have read end to end.

- [ ] The scope lists every page or screen by name.
- [ ] It lists every integration: payment gateway, WhatsApp, CRM, email tool, maps, anything the site must talk to.
- [ ] It states what is not included, in writing.
- [ ] It states who writes and supplies the content: you or the studio.
- [ ] It states how changes and revisions are handled, and what a change will cost.
- [ ] You have replied to it in writing with your agreement, not just nodded on a call.

Red flag: the scope is described as "a standard business website" with no page list attached.

## Design

What you should be holding: design files or screens you can open yourself, at your own pace.

- [ ] Designs use your real content, not lorem ipsum or placeholder text.
- [ ] Every page named in the scope has a design.
- [ ] You have seen the mobile layout, not only the desktop one.
- [ ] Colours, fonts, and logo match your brand.
- [ ] Buttons, menus, and forms carry the actual words that will be used on the live site.
- [ ] You have approved the design in writing. An email or message reply counts.

Red flag: you are asked to approve the design from a screen-share, with nothing left in your hands to review.

## Build (staging)

What you should be holding: a staging link that opens on your own phone and computer.

- [ ] The staging URL loads on your phone and on a desktop browser.
- [ ] Every page from the scope exists and is reachable from the menu.
- [ ] Forms submit properly, and the enquiry lands in the right inbox. Test this with your own email address.
- [ ] Links, buttons, WhatsApp click-to-chat, and phone numbers all work.
- [ ] Images load, and no placeholder text is left anywhere.
- [ ] The payment or checkout flow, if it is in scope, works in test mode.

Red flag: you are shown a video or screenshots of the site instead of a link you can click yourself.

## Launch

What you should be holding: confirmation of go-live, plus the logins that prove ownership.

- [ ] The site is live on your domain, and the domain is registered in your name or your company's name.
- [ ] The hosting account and its billing are in your name, even if the studio manages it for you.
- [ ] Analytics is installed, and you have your own login to see it.
- [ ] Important pages from your old site redirect correctly, if you had one.
- [ ] Forms on the live site deliver to the right inbox. Test again after launch.
- [ ] The site shows the secure lock (HTTPS) in the browser.

Red flag: the site is live, but the domain and hosting sit in the studio's personal account.

## After launch

What you should be holding: full admin access and a written record of how the site is run.

- [ ] You have admin access to the website dashboard, not only editor access.
- [ ] You have a list of every service the site depends on (hosting, domain, email, plugins, licences) and who pays for each one.
- [ ] Backups are running, and you know where they are stored and how to request a restore.
- [ ] Passwords have been handed over, and you have changed them to your own.
- [ ] You have been shown how to make basic edits, or it is agreed in writing who makes them.
- [ ] Any support or maintenance arrangement is written down, including what it covers and what it does not.

Red flag: you cannot log in to your own site's dashboard after launch and have to ask the studio for every small change.

## How to use this

Copy this file into your project folder. Before you approve any stage, open it and tick the boxes for that stage. If anything is unticked, do not settle it on a call. Write it into your approval email as a condition: "Approved, subject to this being done." Release the payment when it is done. A written trail protects you far better than a verbal promise.
